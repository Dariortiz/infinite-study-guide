Analysis · infinite study guide

Darío's ANA MOOC

Limits become spaces; convergence becomes a tool.

A video-first spine assembled from Francis Su's Harvey Mudd Real Analysis lectures, an IMPA Master's-level Measure Theory sequence, Casey Rodriguez's MIT 18.102 Functional Analysis, and a Purdue/NPTEL/Melrose run on Fourier transforms and tempered distributions, backed by Abbott, Folland, and Kreyszig. The order is deliberate: metric-space completeness before measure, measure before operator theory, operator theory before the Fourier transform is extended to distributions; complex analysis (Ahlfors) sits last as optional machinery for residues and asymptotics, not a load-bearing prerequisite.

Choose your route

There is no single course that carries limits from metric-space compactness through Lebesgue integration to compact-operator spectra and Fourier duality without changing conventions along the way. Su's Harvey Mudd lectures and the IMPA measure-theory sequence supply the real-analysis and measure spine; Casey Rodriguez's MIT 18.102 carries it into Banach/Hilbert operator theory, and the Purdue/NPTEL/Melrose sequence extends the transform to tempered distributions.

EXP

Analysis explorer

Take Su's metric-space arc, the IMPA measure sequence, Casey Rodriguez's core operator lectures, and the Fourier/distributions module through Melrose's notes; skip the optional Ahlfors-driven complex-analysis extras.

MODULE 00

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Convergence atlas

  1. Build examples separating pointwise, uniform, almost-everywhere, in-measure, Lp, and weak convergence.
  2. For every implication claimed, prove it or state the missing hypothesis.
  3. Attach plots only after the proof map is complete.

Operator-to-PDE dossier

  1. Choose a compact integral operator arising from a boundary-value or diffusion problem.
  2. Prove boundedness and compactness under stated assumptions.
  3. Discretize it, study spectral convergence, and defend all approximation choices.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: separate the definition from the convergence or approximation theorem it sets up.
  • Derive: reprove one theorem's hypotheses by hand and name a counterexample for a dropped one.
  • Build: implement one discretization, interchange-of-limit check, or transform computation.
  • Explain: state exactly which mode of convergence or which hypothesis a result actually needs.

Minimum portfolio

  • Contraction-mapping existence proof plus a convergence-mode counterexample atlas.
  • A constructed measure with a theorem-by-theorem justified interchange of limit and integral.
  • A compact integral operator discretized and reconciled against the spectral theorem.
  • A distributional Fourier solution of the heat equation.
  • One real integral evaluated by residues with a defended contour choice.

Exit criteria

Real analysis
You can prove the contraction mapping theorem from metric-space completeness and separate pointwise from uniform convergence with a working counterexample.
Measure and integration
You can build the Lebesgue integral from simple functions, audit the hypotheses behind monotone convergence, Fatou, dominated convergence, and Fubini/Tonelli, and compute a Radon–Nikodym derivative.
Functional analysis
You can prove boundedness and compactness for an operator, apply Hahn–Banach, and diagonalize a compact self-adjoint operator via the spectral theorem.
Fourier and distributions
You can prove the Fourier transform preserves Schwartz space, extend it to tempered distributions by duality, and solve a PDE with a distributional initial condition.
Place in the sequence
This guide does not build probability, algebraic structure, or computability. The Hilbert-space and L2-projection geometry from Module 02 is exactly what the PRB guide's conditional-expectation module reuses, and per the Foundations Appendix bridge map the compactness and fixed-point results developed here are what the Motion campus consumes downstream.
