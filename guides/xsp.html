Darío's XSP MOOC

Where classical stochastic calculus stops being enough.

Seven independent frontiers past classical Itô calculus, threaded largely through Martin Hairer's own lecture and note archive: his SPDE minicourse and arXiv notes, his rough-paths course co-written with Peter Friz, and his regularity-structures minicourse sit beside Gregory Lawler's SLE lectures, an ICTP random-matrix course, a Fields Institute talk on quantum Markov semigroups, and François Huveneers's talk on random walk in a random environment. None of the seven modules is part of the classical STO requirement; this guide only opens once that spine, and the analysis behind it, is done.

Three ways classical stochastic calculus runs out: infinite-dimensional noise needs a mild solution instead of an SDE, an irregular path needs an iterated-integral signature instead of Itô's formula, and a conformally invariant curve needs a Loewner driving function instead of a diffusion.

Choose your route

There is no single course spanning SPDEs, rough paths, regularity structures, SLE, random matrices, quantum Markov semigroups, and random environments. Hairer's own lecture and note archive supplies three of the seven modules outright, and Friz, Lawler, Tao, and Watrous supply the rest.

MODULE 00

SPDE

Add infinite-dimensional noise, semigroups, and weak solution concepts.

Stochastic Partial Differential Equations — Lecture 1
Martin Hairer · Лекториум minicourse · 90 min

Hairer begins from the infinite-dimensional noise and analytic machinery needed to make classical SPDE solution concepts precise.

After watching: Derive the stochastic heat equation's mild form and state the topology used by a spectral truncation.
▶ Watch
An Introduction to Stochastic PDEs
Martin Hairer · arXiv lecture notes

Builds cylindrical Wiener processes, semigroup solutions, and regularity for classical SPDEs.

After reading: Derive the mild solution and test a spectral truncation numerically.
PDF
Exit artifact: Analyze and simulate a linear stochastic heat equation with a stated discretization topology.
MODULE 01

Rough Paths

Enhance irregular signals so differential equations depend continuously on their drivers.

Rough Paths — Part 2
Peter Friz · Institut Henri Poincaré · 103 min

A recorded minicourse lecture develops the enhanced-path viewpoint and continuity mechanism behind rough differential equations.

After watching: Compute level-two signatures for two smooth paths and test continuity under a rough-path metric.
▶ Watch
A Course on Rough Paths — Chapters 1–4
Friz & Hairer · Springer · 2nd ed.

Introduces signatures, p-variation, controlled equations, and the universal limit theorem.

After reading: Calculate level-two signatures and solve a simple controlled differential equation.
Read
Exit artifact: Compute iterated integrals for smooth approximations and test pathwise stability.
MODULE 02

Regularity Structures

Renormalize singular SPDEs beyond classical products of distributions.

Introduction to Regularity Structures — Minicourse 1
Martin Hairer · Sydney Mathematical Research Institute · 78 min

Hairer's first minicourse lecture motivates models and reconstruction from the singular products that defeat classical SPDE analysis.

After watching: Build a concept map from scaling through modelled distributions and mark the role of reconstruction.
▶ Watch
Introduction to Regularity Structures
Martin Hairer · arXiv lecture notes

A guided entry to models, reconstruction, and singular SPDE motivation.

After reading: Work a toy reconstruction example and identify the divergent product requiring renormalization.
PDF
Exit artifact: Produce a concept map from local models through reconstruction and renormalization.
MODULE 03

SLE

Describe random conformally invariant curves through Loewner evolution.

An Introduction to the Schramm–Loewner Evolution — Lecture 1
Gregory Lawler · Лекториум minicourse · 57 min

A specific introductory lecture replaces the workshop listing and derives the Loewner evolution used to define SLE.

After watching: Implement chordal Loewner evolution for a discretized Brownian driver and check scaling numerically.
▶ Watch
Conformally Invariant Processes in the Plane — Chapters 3–6
Gregory Lawler · AMS

Develops Loewner chains, Brownian driving, and basic SLE geometry.

After reading: Implement discrete Loewner evolution and numerically check scaling.
Read
Exit artifact: Simulate chordal SLE and derive one scaling or martingale property.

After watching: Simulate Wigner matrices and compare empirical moments with the semicircle moments.

MODULE 05

Quantum Markov

Replace probability measures by states and stochastic maps by quantum channels.

Quantum Markov Semigroups and Noncommutative Curvature
Fields Institute · research lecture · 19 min

A concise research bridge shows how quantum Markov semigroups replace classical transition operators and where entropy enters.

After watching: Construct a qubit depolarizing semigroup, compute its fixed state, and verify complete positivity at sampled times.
▶ Watch
The Theory of Quantum Information — Chapters 2 and 4
John Watrous · author PDF

Gives channels, measurements, distance, and finite-dimensional operator language.

After reading: Implement a qubit channel, compute its Choi matrix, and verify complete positivity.
PDF
Exit artifact: Analyze a finite-dimensional quantum channel and its fixed states.
MODULE 06

Random Environments

Let the medium itself be random and distinguish quenched from annealed laws.

Random Walk in a One-Dimensional Diffusive Random Environment
François Huveneers · ICTS · 47 min

The talk makes the random medium and quenched-versus-annealed viewpoints concrete in one dimension.

After watching: Simulate fixed-environment and environment-averaged trajectories and compare displacement statistics.
▶ Watch
Random Walks in Random Environment
Ofer Zeitouni · Saint-Flour lecture notes · Springer LNM 1837

The published notes replace the dead author-PDF link and remain the theorem-level source for recurrence, speed, and limit laws.

After reading: Derive Solomon's criterion in a simple case and test it by simulation.
Read
Exit artifact: Simulate a one-dimensional random walk in random environment and compare quenched/annealed observables.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Beyond-Itô boundary report

  1. Choose one process class beyond classical finite-dimensional SDEs.
  2. State the new object, topology, and failure of the classical framework.
  3. Reproduce one proof mechanism and one computation.
  4. Connect prerequisites back to STO, ANA, or ALG.

Singular-equation capstone

  1. Complete both the rough-paths and regularity-structures exit artifacts.
  2. Explain, in one page, how a rough path's iterated-integral signature becomes a regularity structure's "model" once the driving noise stops being a finite-dimensional path.
  3. Identify one divergent product each formalism renormalizes differently.
  4. State which topology — p-variation, or the reconstruction theorem's Hölder-type norm — makes each continuity claim precise.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: identify exactly which classical tool — Itô's formula, a generator, a martingale property — stops applying.
  • Derive: reproduce one identity or estimate by hand: a mild solution, a signature, a Loewner ODE, or a semicircle moment.
  • Simulate: implement one numerical experiment matching that module's exit artifact.
  • Explain: state the new object or topology that replaces the classical one.

Minimum portfolio

  • Mild-solution simulation of a linear stochastic heat equation with its discretization topology stated (Module 00).
  • Level-two signature computation with a rough-path-metric continuity test (Module 01).
  • Concept map from local models through reconstruction and renormalization (Module 02).
  • Chordal SLE simulation with one proved scaling or martingale property (Module 03).
  • Wigner-matrix simulation compared against the semicircle law (Module 04).
  • Qubit channel with its Choi matrix and a complete-positivity check (Module 05).
  • One-dimensional RWRE simulation comparing quenched and annealed observables (Module 06).

Exit criteria

SPDEs and rough paths
You can derive a mild solution for a linear stochastic heat equation and state its discretization topology, and compute level-two signatures while testing continuity under a rough-path metric.
Regularity structures
You can build a concept map from scaling through modelled distributions to reconstruction, and identify the divergent product a given singular SPDE needs renormalized.
SLE and random matrices
You can implement chordal Loewner evolution for a discretized driver and derive one SLE scaling property, and simulate a Wigner ensemble and compare its empirical spectrum against the semicircle law.
Quantum Markov semigroups and random environments
You can construct a finite-dimensional quantum channel, compute its Choi matrix, and verify complete positivity; and you can distinguish quenched from annealed laws for a random walk in a random environment.
Place in the sequence
You can point out that this guide does not build classical Itô calculus, generators, or filtrations from scratch — that stays the STO guide — and does not build the functional-analytic machinery (topologies, spaces, distributions) it constantly assumes — that stays the ANA guide.

Curation policy

Scope: seven independent frontiers past classical, finite-dimensional stochastic calculus — SPDEs, rough paths, regularity structures, SLE, random matrices, quantum Markov semigroups, and random walk in random environment. Classical process theory — laws, filtrations, generators — is the STO guide; the functional-analytic machinery behind it is the ANA guide. Named lecture archives and standard references only; no unfiltered channel archives.
