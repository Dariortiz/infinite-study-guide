MODULE 00

Length Leakage

Model ciphertext length as an explicit observation channel. AEAD mechanics remain in Session/E2E.

Compression Oracle Attacks on VPN Networks
Velimir Radlovački · Black Hat USA · 44 min

Shows how compressed length becomes an observable channel in a deployed encrypted tunnel, making the leakage function concrete.

After watching: Define the adversary's observations and estimate leakage on a controlled corpus.
▶ Watch
Compression and Information Leakage of Plaintext
John Kelsey · FSE 2002 · paper

Frames compression ratio and compressed length as information available to an adversary.

After reading: Define the adversary's observations and estimate leakage on a controlled corpus.
PDF
Symmetric Encryption: Record Layer Security
Kenny Paterson · Bar-Ilan Winter School on Cryptography · 50 min

A research-level record-layer lecture makes the encryption boundary, per-record metadata, and attacker-visible lengths explicit before the prerequisite guide.

After watching: Write the record syntax and mark every field or length observable to an on-path adversary.
▶ Watch
Session/E2E guide — authenticated-encryption prerequisite
Existing C7 guide · prerequisite pointer

SCX assumes nonce, integrity, record, and transcript literacy; it does not re-teach AEAD.

After reading: Pass the Session/E2E AEAD and record-layer checkpoints, then return with a concrete length function.
Open guide
Exit artifact: Write a leakage function for a padded messaging format and quantify distinguishability among candidate plaintext classes.
MODULE 01

Compression Oracles

Study chosen-input adaptation when attacker-controlled and secret bytes share one compression context.

The CRIME Attack
Juliano Rizzo & Thai Duong · ekoparty 2012 · 98 min

The original presenters explain adaptive compression leakage across encrypted web records and the assumptions behind exploitation.

After watching: Implement only a synthetic local oracle; vary noise, padding, and context separation.
▶ Watch
SSL, Gone in 30 Seconds: A BREACH Beyond CRIME
Yoel Gluck, Neal Harris & Angelo Prado · Black Hat · 56 min

The conference recording demonstrates how chosen reflection and response compression survive transport encryption.

After watching: Build an assumption/mitigation table from the attack narrative.
▶ Watch
BREACH: Reviving the CRIME Attack
Gluck, Harris & Prado · Black Hat USA 2013 · paper

Shows that response compression and chosen reflection can leak secrets despite transport encryption.

After reading: Produce an assumption/mitigation table; point protocol details back to Session/E2E.
PDF
Exit artifact: Build a toy local oracle, recover a synthetic secret, and list every assumption required for the attack.
MODULE 02

Ciphertext Incompressibility

Use successful ciphertext compression as a distinguisher, not as an optimization target.

The Pseudorandomness Toolkit
Salil Vadhan · Simons Institute · 86 min

Builds the computational indistinguishability language needed to treat successful ciphertext compression as a distinguisher.

After watching: Turn a length-saving compressor into a distinguisher and bound its advantage.
▶ Watch
Katz–Lindell — computational security and pseudorandomness chapters
Jonathan Katz & Yehuda Lindell · CRC Press · revised 3rd ed.

The written companion states games, reductions, and advantage bounds precisely.

After reading: Write the compressor distinguisher as a formal security game.
Read
Exit artifact: Test a compressor on random bytes, secure ciphertext, and deliberately flawed envelopes with proper confidence intervals.
MODULE 03

Extractors Are Not Compressors

Separate min-entropy extraction from model-based removal of Shannon redundancy.

Randomness Extraction: A Survey
David Zuckerman · Institute for Advanced Study · 105 min

A theorem-level survey separates min-entropy extraction from removal of Shannon redundancy.

After watching: Work one leftover-hash parameter calculation and contrast it with a source-coding bound.
▶ Watch
Randomness Extraction from Somewhat Dependent Sources
Xin Li · Simons Institute · 31 min

Shows why source assumptions, not compressibility, control extractor guarantees.

After watching: Construct two source families with similar Shannon entropy but different min-entropy.
▶ Watch
Pseudorandomness — Chapter 6, Randomness Extractors
Salil Vadhan · Foundations and Trends · author PDF

The written reference states seeded-extractor and leftover-hash guarantees against explicit min-entropy assumptions.

After reading: Write a precise extractor definition and compare it with a source-code guarantee.
PDF
Exit artifact: Create two source families with similar Shannon entropy but different min-entropy and explain the incompatible guarantees.
MODULE 04

Encrypted Deduplication

Understand equality and confirmation leakage introduced by convergent encryption and client-side deduplication.

Security and deduplication in the cloud
Danny Harnik · IBM Research · Technion lecture · 46 min

Harnik's own work established the deduplication side channel, and the lecture walks the confirmation-of-file and cross-user leakage attacks before naming the defenses.

After watching: Reproduce the response-time distinguisher from the talk on a toy client-side deduplication design.
▶ Watch
Message-Locked Encryption and Secure Deduplication
Bellare, Keelveedhi & Ristenpart · EUROCRYPT 2013

The formal counterpart to the lecture: it defines MLE and pins the security claim to message unpredictability.

After reading: Analyze a low-entropy file space and test an offline confirmation attack against a toy design.
PDF
Exit artifact: Threat-model an encrypted backup design, including equality, length, membership, and access-pattern leakage.
MODULE 05

Resource & Timing Oracles

Treat decompression work, allocation, and table access as adversarially observable behavior.

Time Trial: Racing Towards Practical Timing Attacks
Daniel Mayer · Black Hat · 50 min

Demonstrates the measurement discipline and noise controls needed before claiming a timing oracle.

After watching: Audit an entropy decoder's lookup and branch behavior and quantify timing classes.
▶ Watch
CacheBleed: A Timing Attack on Constant-Time RSA
Yuval Yarom · IACR · 21 min

Shows that resource contention can defeat simplistic constant-time claims even without secret-dependent cache-line addresses.

After watching: Add allocation, recursion, and time budgets to a decoder harness.
▶ Watch
I Came to Drop Bombs: Auditing the Compression Algorithm Weapon Cache
Cara Marie · Black Hat USA 2016 · 28 min

Audits decompression bombs across archive and stream formats and turns expansion, parser, and resource limits into concrete defensive requirements.

After watching: Build a matrix of each demonstrated bomb class, exhausted resource, and enforceable decoder budget.
▶ Watch
A Better Zip Bomb
David Fifield · technical article · 2019

The implementation companion makes extreme expansion and parser composition concrete without conflating denial of service with cryptanalysis.

After reading: Enforce compressed-size, expanded-size, ratio, depth, and time limits independently.
Read
Exit artifact: Harden a decoder harness with input, output, memory, recursion, and time budgets; measure residual timing classes.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Length-safe composition review

  1. Choose a compressed encrypted application and link its transport assumptions to Session/E2E.
  2. Specify attacker control, observations, secret distribution, and adaptive queries.
  3. Reproduce leakage only in a local synthetic harness.
  4. Evaluate context separation, padding, rate limits, and resource bounds without claiming perfect concealment.
