Number Theory · infinite study guide

Darío's NUM MOOC

Arithmetic as algebra, analysis, probability, and motion.

A video-first spine built from Richard Borcherds's Berkeley Math 115 lectures, U. K. Anandavardhanan's IIT Bombay MA524 algebraic number theory course, Rodrigo Hernández-Gutiérrez's UAM Teoría de Números lectures, Kaneenika Sinha's IISER Pune probabilistic number theory course, and Joseph Silverman's arithmetic-dynamics survey and text, backed by Ireland–Rosen, Marcus, Gouvêa, Apostol, Tenenbaum, and Silverman. The order moves from elementary congruences and reciprocity through algebraic number fields, local/p-adic completions, analytic density results, probabilistic limit laws, and arithmetic dynamics. The ring- and ideal-theoretic machinery is ALG's job, not rebuilt here; this guide hands the ideal class group's embedded-lattice picture on to LAT once Minkowski's bound is computed.

Reciprocity governs how a single prime behaves; factoring (p) into prime ideals shows how that behavior splits once the base ring grows; the Euler product turns the whole set of primes into one analytic object; and Erdős–Kac shows the same primes, counted statistically, obey a normal law.

Choose your route

There's no single course that carries congruences to ideal class groups to prime-counting to Erdős–Kac without switching authors. Borcherds's Berkeley 115 supplies the elementary, local, and analytic spine; Anandavardhanan's IIT Bombay course carries the algebraic side; Sinha's IISER Pune lectures add the probabilistic layer; Silverman closes with arithmetic dynamics.

EXP

Arithmetic explorer

Follow Borcherds through elementary and analytic number theory, Anandavardhanan's opening algebraic lectures, and Gaussian-integer factorization; finish one proof artifact per module.

8–10 h video + reading
BLD

Balanced builder

Complete all core units and artifacts, including the quadratic-field ring-of-integers computation, p-adic Hensel lifting, an Euler-product prime-counting comparison, an Erdős–Kac experiment, and an arithmetic-dynamics orbit explorer.

18–26 h + reading
DIP

Deep dive

Read deeper into Marcus's ideal theory, work through Sinha's full 18-lecture probabilistic number theory course, and add Silverman's dynamical-systems chapters past periodic points; stop before class field theory unless it becomes a new specialization.

35–50 h + reading

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

One quadratic field, end to end

  1. Determine its ring of integers, integral basis, discriminant, and unit evidence.
  2. Factor several rational primes and explain splitting through minimal polynomials and ideals.
  3. Use Minkowski's bound to constrain the class group, then hand the embedded lattice viewpoint to LAT.
  4. Deliver reproducible computations plus proofs that identify every theorem used.

Arithmetic as probability and motion

  1. Choose continued fractions, additive arithmetic functions, or polynomial iteration.
  2. State the probability space or dynamical system, invariant quantity, and arithmetic observable.
  3. Run a finite experiment with uncertainty or finite-size diagnostics.
  4. Explain which asymptotic theorem supports the pattern and which claims remain heuristic.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: separate the theorem statement from its proof technique.
  • Derive: reprove one CRT, reciprocity, or Euler-product step by hand.
  • Build: run one arithmetic experiment — Hensel lift, Erdős–Kac count, or orbit iteration.
  • Explain: state which claims are proved and which remain heuristic or asymptotic.

Minimum portfolio

  • Quadratic-field ring-of-integers and Minkowski-bound calculator.
  • Hensel-lifting p-adic root finder with logged precision at every iteration.
  • Euler-product prime-counting comparison against π(x) and Li(x).
  • Erdős–Kac experiment with normalization and finite-size discussion.
  • Rational-map orbit explorer over Q and finite fields.

Exit criteria

Elementary and algebraic number theory
You can prove CRT and quadratic reciprocity, then compute a quadratic field's ring of integers, discriminant, prime splitting, and a Minkowski-bound estimate for its class group.
Local and p-adic methods
You can construct Q_p from Cauchy sequences, apply Hensel's lemma to lift a root, and explain why p-adic convergence differs from convergence in the real metric.
Analytic number theory
You can derive an Euler product from a Dirichlet series and state precisely what the Prime Number Theorem does and does not bound about π(x).
Probabilistic number theory
You can state normal order and the Turán–Kubilius inequality, then run and interpret an Erdős–Kac experiment on the number of distinct prime factors.
Arithmetic dynamics
You can iterate a rational map over Q and over finite fields, track periodic points and heights, and name one local-global question the data alone cannot settle.
Place in the sequence
You can point to ALG for the ring and ideal machinery this guide reuses rather than rebuilding, and you hand the ideal class group's embedded-lattice picture to LAT once Minkowski's bound is computed.
