Dynamics: Second Pass · infinite study guide

Darío's XDS MOOC

Global geometry and infinite-dimensional motion.

Five self-contained excursions past a first dynamical-systems course, each built from one recorded lecture and one standard reference: Berglund's KAM tutorial against Pöschel's proof notes, Osborne's symplectic-geometry course against Cannas da Silva's lecture notes, a Fields Institute talk on Conley theory against Kaczynski–Mischaikow–Mrozek's computational homology, Drivas's turbulence minicourse against Temam's infinite-dimensional dynamical systems, and Juliette Bruce's complex-dynamics lecture against Milnor's Dynamics in One Complex Variable. None of the five modules is required by DYN or DSZ; each only makes sense once that first pass is complete.

Three different notions of invariance: a symplectic form turns a Hamiltonian into a flow that preserves it, a Diophantine condition protects a torus from resonance under that flow, and a Julia set is the invariant boundary between escape and capture once the map stops being linear.

Choose your route

There is no single course spanning KAM theory, symplectic geometry, Conley index theory, Navier–Stokes as an infinite-dimensional flow, and complex dynamics. Berglund, Osborne, the Fields Institute program, Drivas, and Bruce supply the lecture spine; Pöschel, Cannas da Silva, Kaczynski–Mischaikow–Mrozek, Temam, and Milnor are the proof-level companions.

MODULE 00

KAM in Depth

Study persistence of invariant tori through small divisors and iterative normal forms.

KAM Theory and the Stability of the Solar System
Nils Berglund · KAM tutorial · 78 min

The tutorial connects Diophantine frequencies, perturbation size, and invariant-torus persistence to a concrete Hamiltonian system.

After watching: Solve one homological equation and visualize resonant gaps in a standard-map parameter sweep.
▶ Watch
A Lecture on the Classical KAM Theorem
Jürgen Pöschel · AMS proceedings

A concise proof route through Diophantine conditions and the iteration.

After reading: Carry out one homological-equation step and track the small-divisor estimate.
Read
Exit artifact: Work a finite KAM iteration for a near-integrable map and visualize surviving tori.
MODULE 01

Symplectic Geometry

Use symplectic manifolds, Hamiltonian vector fields, and moment maps as dynamics language.

Symplectic Geometry and Classical Mechanics — Lecture 1
Tobias Osborne · recorded lecture course · 85 min

Begins from Hamiltonian mechanics and derives the symplectic structures used throughout the geometric module.

After watching: Compute the Hamiltonian vector field for two explicit systems and verify flow preservation of the symplectic form.
▶ Watch
Lectures on Symplectic Geometry
Ana Cannas da Silva · Springer LNM 1764

A direct route from forms and Darboux to Hamiltonian actions and reduction.

After reading: Verify a map is symplectic, derive its Hamiltonian field, and compute a moment map.
Read
Exit artifact: Compute Hamiltonian flows and symplectic reductions for explicit examples.
MODULE 02

Conley Theory

Describe global dynamics with isolating neighborhoods and index invariants.

Conley Theory and the Global Dynamics of Games
Fields Institute · research lecture · 33 min

A concrete application shows how isolating neighborhoods and Conley-type invariants encode global behavior beyond local linearization.

After watching: Construct an isolating neighborhood for a planar flow and compute the homology of a candidate index pair.
▶ Watch
Computational Homology
Kaczynski, Mischaikow & Mrozek · Springer

Connects cubical homology and rigorous computation to Conley-index methods.

After reading: Build a cubical enclosure for a map and calculate homology of an index pair.
Read
Exit artifact: Compute a Conley index for a planar isolated invariant set and validate it numerically.
MODULE 03

Navier–Stokes as Dynamics

Treat dissipative PDE evolution, absorbing sets, and attractors as an infinite-dimensional flow.

Mathematical Aspects of Turbulence — Part I
Theodore Drivas · Simons Center minicourse · 88 min

The minicourse frames Navier–Stokes through energy transfer and turbulent dynamics, supplying the analytic interpretation needed for the spectral artifact.

After watching: Implement a dealiased 2D pseudospectral solver and audit its discrete energy and enstrophy balances.
▶ Watch
Infinite-Dimensional Dynamical Systems — selected chapters
Roger Temam · Springer

Supplies semigroup, dissipativity, and global-attractor language for PDE dynamics.

After reading: Prove an absorbing-ball estimate for a simplified dissipative equation.
Read
Exit artifact: Implement a spectral 2D flow and estimate energy/enstrophy balances and attractor diagnostics.
MODULE 04

Complex Dynamics in Depth

Move from iteration pictures to normal families, Julia sets, and parameter space.

Complex Dynamics — Lecture 1
Juliette Bruce · MMUSSL lecture · 59 min

A full introductory lecture moves from iteration to Fatou and Julia behavior rather than stopping at rendered fractals.

After watching: Render a polynomial Julia set and prove complete invariance for the same map.
▶ Watch
Dynamics in One Complex Variable — Chapters 4–14
John Milnor · Princeton · 3rd ed.

The standard rigorous route through rational dynamics and Julia sets.

After reading: Prove complete invariance of the Julia set and implement interval-guarded escape tests.
Read
Exit artifact: Render certified escape sets and prove one structural statement about Julia/Fatou behavior.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Global-dynamics dossier

  1. Choose one optional global or infinite-dimensional setting.
  2. State the phase space, invariant object, and topology.
  3. Prove one structural claim and implement one validated computation.
  4. Map prerequisites to DYN, ALG, ANA, and numerical guides.

Hamiltonian capstone

  1. Complete both the KAM and symplectic-geometry exit artifacts.
  2. Confirm numerically that the near-integrable map from your KAM iteration is a section of the Hamiltonian flow whose symplectic form you computed in Module 01.
  3. Identify the Diophantine condition as a nonresonance requirement on that flow's frequencies.
  4. State one persistence obstruction that appears once the Diophantine condition fails.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: locate exactly which invariant — a torus, a symplectic form, an index pair, an energy balance, a Julia set — the lecture is protecting or computing.
  • Derive: reproduce one estimate by hand: a homological-equation step, a moment map, an index-pair homology, or an absorbing-ball estimate.
  • Simulate: implement that module's numerical artifact and check it against the proved statement.
  • Explain: state what a first dynamical-systems course could not yet prove about this object.

Minimum portfolio

  • Finite KAM iteration for a near-integrable map with surviving tori visualized (Module 00).
  • Hamiltonian flow and symplectic reduction for an explicit system (Module 01).
  • Conley index for a planar isolated invariant set, validated numerically (Module 02).
  • Dealiased 2D pseudospectral solver with audited energy and enstrophy balances (Module 03).
  • Certified escape-set rendering with one proved Julia/Fatou statement (Module 04).

Exit criteria

KAM in depth
You can carry out a homological-equation step for a near-integrable Hamiltonian map, track its small-divisor estimate, and identify which invariant tori survive a standard-map parameter sweep.
Symplectic geometry
You can verify a map is symplectic, derive its Hamiltonian vector field, compute a moment map, and carry out a symplectic reduction on an explicit example.
Conley theory
You can construct an isolating neighborhood for a planar flow, build a cubical enclosure, and compute the Conley index of an index pair from its homology.
Navier–Stokes as dynamics
You can implement a dealiased 2D pseudospectral solver, audit its discrete energy and enstrophy balances, and state an absorbing-ball estimate for a simplified dissipative equation.
Complex dynamics in depth
You can render a certified polynomial Julia set, prove its complete invariance, and implement interval-guarded escape tests.
Place in the sequence
You can point out that this guide does not build a first dynamical-systems course's local theory — fixed points, linearization, bifurcation, a first pass at flows and maps — from scratch; that stays the DYN guide and the DSZ guide this bundle already requires before opening.
