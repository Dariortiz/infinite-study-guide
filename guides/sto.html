MODULE 00

Starts from transition matrices but keeps recurrence and steady state tied to sample paths.

Markov Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors
Robert Gallager · MIT 6.262 Lecture 8 · 84 min

Explains why the spectral gap, not the stationary vector alone, is what governs the rate of convergence.

After watching: Compute the second-largest eigenvalue modulus of your chain and compare its predicted decay with the measured mixing curve.
▶ Watch

J. R. Norris · Cambridge University Press · 1997 · read

Supplies the countable-state recurrence, hitting-time, invariant-measure, and convergence proofs behind the computational lectures.

Read

A Spanish-language computational pass over powers of P and stationary behavior, paired with the course's open notebooks.

Martingales: Plain, Sub, and Super
Robert Gallager · MIT 6.262 Lecture 23 · 83 min

Develops martingales from random walks and sequential decisions before using stopping arguments.

After watching: Construct the Doob martingale of a revealed sum and verify the conditional-expectation identity numerically.
▶ Watch
Martingales: Stopping and Converging
Robert Gallager · MIT 6.262 Lecture 24 · 81 min

Separates valid stopping arguments from the seductive but false unrestricted version.

After watching: Choose a bounded stopping time, prove optional stopping for it, and compare the theorem with a doubling-strategy counterexample.
▶ Watch
Probability Measure Martingales: Vitali’s convergence theorem, martingale inequalities: Yr 3 Lecture
Oxford Mathematics · 54 min

Develops martingale inequalities and convergence at the same measure-theoretic level as Durrett's chapter, including the uniform-integrability mechanism behind L¹ convergence.

After watching: Extract the lecture's convergence hypotheses, then test them on one nonnegative martingale and one almost-surely convergent counterexample.
▶ Watch
Durrett, Probability: Theory and Examples — Chapter 5
Rick Durrett · 5th ed. author PDF · Chapter 5 · read

Adds convergence theorems, maximal inequalities, and uniformly integrable martingales at the measure-theoretic level.

PDF

MODULE 03

Generators & Semigroups

Use generators as infinitesimal models and semigroups as the law's time evolution; keep domains visible.

The Markov Generator
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · 39 min

Derives the generator as the derivative of the semigroup at zero, which is the step that makes the domain question unavoidable.

After watching: For a finite Q-matrix, verify the semigroup law and recover Q as the derivative at zero.
▶ Watch
Bounded Generators
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · 24 min

Shows what the bounded case buys you—exponentiation of the generator—and therefore what breaks without it.

After watching: Exponentiate a bounded generator to recover the semigroup, then identify where the series argument fails for an unbounded example.
▶ Watch
Ethier–Kurtz, Markov Processes — Chapters 1 and 4
Stewart Ethier & Thomas Kurtz · Wiley · 1986 · read

Establishes the generator/semigroup language used for Markov-process characterization and approximation, including martingale problems.

After reading: Show that f(Xₜ)−f(X₀)−∫Lf(Xₛ)ds is a martingale for one finite chain and one diffusion test function.
Read
Generator of a Feller Semigroup and Resolvent
Max von Renesse · Stochastic Processes Lecture 28 · 67 min

Directly develops Feller-semigroup generators, resolvents, and the Brownian generator before the optional Liggett treatment.

After watching: Recover the Brownian generator from the semigroup and write the corresponding resolvent identity.
▶ Watch
Liggett, Continuous Time Markov Processes — Chapters 2–3
Thomas Liggett · AMS Graduate Studies in Mathematics 113 · 2010 · read

Optional Feller-semigroup depth: strong continuity, Hille–Yosida intuition, and construction from generators.

After reading: Write a one-page domain audit for a candidate generator: space, core, positive maximum principle, and conservativity.
Read
Exit artifact: Derive a generator three ways—from a transition law, an SDE, and a simulation—and state its domain assumptions.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Generator-to-simulator dossier

  1. Choose a CTMC or diffusion and state its filtration, state space, generator, and domain.
  2. Construct it by simulation and independently through its martingale problem or Kolmogorov equation.
  3. Estimate one invariant or hitting-time quantity, attach uncertainty, and reconcile it with theory.
  4. Document every approximation, convergence claim, and failure mode in a reproducible notebook.

Functional-limit study

  1. Choose a scaled random walk, renewal count, or queue-length process.
  2. State the candidate limit and topology; prove finite-dimensional convergence and give the tightness argument.
  3. Test a continuous path functional numerically and identify where the mapping theorem is used.
  4. Deliver a ten-minute proof talk plus code that regenerates every figure.
