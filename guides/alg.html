Algebra & Spectra · infinite study guide

Darío's ALG MOOC

Structure first; computation follows.

A non-centralized course built from Daniel Labardini's UNAM Álgebra Moderna lectures, Gilbert Strang's MIT 18.06, and Richard Borcherds's Berkeley representation-theory and topology series, backed by Artin, Axler, Fulton–Harris, and Hatcher. The order is deliberate: structure before computation, computation before representation, representation before topology. Algebraic number theory's use of rings and ideals is the NUM guide; this one builds the machinery, not the arithmetic.

Structure comes first: a homomorphism's kernel is already a normal subgroup before any matrix gets diagonalized. Spectral theory turns a linear map into eigenvalues; representation theory turns a group action into a matrix, and its character back into a class function.

Choose your route

There is no single course that goes from first isomorphism theorems to character tables to fundamental groups without changing notation halfway through. Labardini and Strang supply the algebraic and spectral spine; Borcherds carries it into representation theory and topology without a re-introduction.

EXP

Structure explorer

Watch Labardini's groups/rings/modules arc and Strang's spectral sequence; skip representation theory and the fundamental group unless a later guide needs them.

7–10 h video + reading

16–22 h + reading

Deep dive

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Structure and canonical form

  1. Choose a finitely generated module or a linear operator over a specified field.
  2. Derive its canonical decomposition from the relevant structure theorem.
  3. Implement a checker and explain why numerical floating-point output is not the proof.

Symmetry-to-spectrum study

  1. Choose a finite group action and form its permutation representation.
  2. Decompose it using characters or invariant subspaces.
  3. Relate the decomposition to the spectrum of one equivariant operator.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: separate the definition from the theorem it motivates.
  • Derive: reprove one structure theorem or character-table row by hand.
  • Build: implement one canonical-form or decomposition checker.
  • Explain: state the invariant a symmetry argument actually proves.

Minimum portfolio

  • Invariant-factor classifier for a finitely generated abelian group or PID-module.
  • Symmetric-matrix diagonalizer with a spectral-theorem proof sketch, plus its SVD.
  • Character table for one group beyond Borcherds's D8/A4/S4/S5/A5 set.
  • Fundamental-group computation for one complex not in the lecture set, via Seifert–van Kampen.

Exit criteria

Groups, rings, and modules
You can state and apply the first and second isomorphism theorems, and classify a finitely generated abelian group or a module over a PID by its invariant factors.
Spectral linear algebra
You can diagonalize a symmetric or normal operator via the spectral theorem, derive the SVD from it, and fall back to Jordan form when diagonalization fails.
Representation theory
You can build a representation from a group action, use character orthogonality to decompose it, and read off a character table for a small group by hand.
Topology for dynamics
You can compute the fundamental group of a cell complex using Seifert–van Kampen, at the level later dynamics and Conley theory actually consume — not full algebraic topology.
Place in the sequence
You can point to where algebraic number theory's use of rings and ideals continues (the NUM guide) instead of re-deriving group and ring structure there.
