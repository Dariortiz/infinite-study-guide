MODULE 00

Probability Spaces

Start from measures, random variables, independence, and laws—not from density manipulation.

σ-Fields
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 27 min

Fixes the event structure as the primitive object so that every later theorem has somewhere to attach its hypotheses.

After watching: Show that the intersection of σ-fields is a σ-field and exhibit two σ-fields whose union is not one.
▶ Watch
Measures: Definition and Examples
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 21 min

Separates the measure axioms from probability's normalization so countable additivity does the work rather than intuition.

After watching: Verify countable additivity for a counting measure and for a Stieltjes measure you define yourself.
▶ Watch
Measurable Functions
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 36 min

Defines random variables as measurable maps, which is the step that makes induced laws and conditioning well posed.

After watching: Prove measurability of a random variable you construct, then push its law forward onto the Borel line.
▶ Watch
Independence
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 38 min

Treats independence as a statement about generated σ-fields, which is what the π–λ extension argument actually needs.

After watching: Prove the π–λ independence extension and give a triple of events that is pairwise but not mutually independent.
▶ Watch
Sigma álgebras, medidas de probabilidad e independencia
ES · Luis Rincón · UNAM Probabilidad II · serie de clases cortas

A Spanish pass over the same foundations; useful for checking whether the concepts survive a change of language and exposition.

After watching: Formalize one lecture example as (Ω,F,P), define its random variables, and prove their measurability in both languages.
▶ Watch
Strong law of large numbers, part 1
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 21 min · English

Targets the strong-law material assigned in Durrett and begins the proof at the same measure-theoretic level as the written chapter.

After watching: State the integrability and independence assumptions precisely, then reconstruct the first proof reduction without appealing to simulation.
▶ Watch
Durrett, Probability: Theory and Examples — Chapters 1–2
Rick Durrett · 5th ed. author PDF · read

The written spine behind the videos: measure foundations, independence, laws of large numbers, and theorem-level hypotheses.

MODULE 01

Conditional Expectation

Define conditioning as a σ-algebra-measurable projection and derive familiar formulas only afterward.

Conditional Expectation (I)
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 27 min

Builds E[X|G] from the defining integral identity rather than from a formula, which is the definition every later theorem uses.

After watching: For a finite partition and its generated σ-algebra, derive E[X|G] and verify the defining identity on every atom.
▶ Watch
Orthogonal Projection
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 29 min

Gives the L2 geometry that makes conditioning a projection, connecting this module to the Hilbert-space work in ANA.

After watching: Compute one conditional expectation as an L2 projection and confirm it matches the Radon–Nikodym construction.
▶ Watch
Conditional MCT, Fatou, DCT, Jensen
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 27 min

Ports the convergence theorems through the conditional operator, which is where most informal arguments quietly break.

After watching: Prove the conditional Jensen inequality and construct a case where dropping integrability defeats it.
▶ Watch
Esperanza condicional: definición y propiedades
ES · Luis Rincón · UNAM Probabilidad II · 24 min

A Spanish theorem-level treatment reinforcing conditioning on information rather than on events or memorized densities.

After watching: Prove the tower property from the defining identity and state it precisely in both languages.
▶ Watch
Regular Conditional Distributions
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 27 min

Covers the kernel and disintegration machinery needed before conditioning on a continuous variable is legitimate.

▶ Watch

MODULE 03

Weak Convergence

Separate convergence of laws from convergence of variables and make tightness the missing compactness principle.

Weak Convergence
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 34 min

Defines convergence of laws through test functions, which is what makes Portmanteau a theorem rather than a definition.

After watching: Prove one direction of Portmanteau and apply it to a normalized-sum example.
▶ Watch
Prokhorov's Compactness Theorem
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 15 min

Supplies the compactness principle: tightness is what lets you extract a convergent subsequence of laws.

After watching: Prove tightness of a family of laws and identify a subsequential limit without invoking a density.
▶ Watch
Skorohod's Theorem
Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 24 min

Converts convergence of laws into almost-sure convergence on a common space, which is how mapping arguments are made rigorous.

After watching: Use a Skorohod representation to prove a continuous mapping result you first believed for the wrong reason.
▶ Watch
Convergencia en distribución y relaciones entre los tipos de convergencia

A Spanish treatment that lays the convergence modes side by side and proves the implications between them.

▶ Watch

Todd Kemp · UCSD Math 280 · measure-theoretic probability · 26 min

Contrasts a strong metric on laws with weak convergence, clarifying which limit theorems need which topology.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Conditioning and information dossier

  1. Specify a filtration generated by sequential observations.
  2. Construct the associated Doob martingale and prove convergence under explicit assumptions.
  3. Compare exact conditional expectations with Monte Carlo estimates and report uncertainty.

Weak-limit defense

  1. Choose a nontrivial sequence of probability laws.
  2. Prove tightness and identify every subsequential limit.
  3. Use characteristic functions or a mapping theorem to establish uniqueness, then present a ten-minute proof talk.
