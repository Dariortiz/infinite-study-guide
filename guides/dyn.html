Dynamical Systems Spine · infinite study guide

Darío's DYN MOOC

Follow the orbit. Explain the change.

A spine assembled from Steven Strogatz's Cornell MAE 5790 lectures, Jason Bramburger's Dynamical Systems and Chaos Theory courses, and Shane Ross's Virginia Tech dynamics sequence, backed by Hirsch–Smale–Devaney, Perko, Khalil, and Guckenheimer–Holmes. The order is deliberate: existence and linear classification come before nonlinear qualitative theory, Lyapunov's direct method before bifurcation, uniform hyperbolicity before ergodic averaging, and finite-dimensional flows before the PDE and delay module that ends the spine. Named example families like the logistic map, the Hénon map, and Smale's horseshoe live in the DSZ guide; this one builds the flow, stability, and bifurcation theory those examples instantiate.

ẋ = f(x), |f(x)−f(y)| ≤ L|x−y|
Df(x*) eigenvalues ⇒ stability
V̇(x) = ∇V(x)·f(x) < 0

Existence and uniqueness come from a Lipschitz bound before any trajectory is drawn; local stability reads off the Jacobian's eigenvalues at an equilibrium, and where that linear test is inconclusive, a Lyapunov function proves stability directly from the vector field.

Choose your route

There is no single course that carries linear classification through Lyapunov proof technique, bifurcation normal forms, and ergodic averaging without changing convention. Strogatz's Cornell lectures supply the qualitative geometric core; Bramburger and Ross extend it into hyperbolicity, symbolic tools, and center-manifold reduction without re-deriving the basics.

EXP

Dynamics explorer

Work through Strogatz's linear and nonlinear planar-systems lectures, Wiggins's Lyapunov and LaSalle arguments, and Bramburger's ergodic-theory unit; skip the PDE/delay module and its infinite-dimensional state spaces.

18–24 h + reading
BLD

Phase-portrait builder

Add Ross's stable/unstable/center-subspace and center-manifold lectures, compute stable and unstable manifolds for a saddle map, and close with the reaction–diffusion or delay-equation artifact.

30–40 h + reading
DIP

Proof deep dive

Read Katok–Hasselblatt's hyperbolic-sets chapters and Brin–Stuck's ergodic-theory chapters in full, prove the stable-manifold theorem and the hypotheses behind Birkhoff's ergodic theorem, and defend the Hale–Verduyn Lunel characteristic equation for a delay model.

45–60 h + reading

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Model-to-invariant dossier

  1. Choose a nonlinear model and state its phase space and flow hypotheses.
  2. Classify equilibria and invariant sets analytically.
  3. Track one bifurcation or global transition numerically.
  4. Deliver reproducible code and a proof map separating evidence from theorem.

Robust dynamics study

  1. Choose a hyperbolic map or flow.
  2. Compute invariant directions and stable/unstable manifolds.
  3. Perturb the model and test the predicted persistence.
  4. Give a ten-minute defense of every robustness claim.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: separate the geometric picture — a phase portrait, a bifurcation diagram — from the theorem that justifies it.
  • Derive: reprove one existence, stability, or normal-form result by hand.
  • Build: produce one phase portrait, Lyapunov certificate, or invariant-manifold approximation numerically.
  • Explain: state which hypothesis — Lipschitz, hyperbolicity, transversality — the claim actually depends on.

Minimum portfolio

  • Proof-and-computation dossier for a nonlinear IVP, with maximal interval and a numerical convergence check.
  • Classification of every real two-dimensional linear flow, including defective and complex-spectrum cases.
  • Verified Lyapunov function for a nonlinear equilibrium with its certified basin mapped numerically.
  • Bifurcation diagram whose numerical branches are matched to a normal-form derivation.
  • Stable and unstable manifolds for a saddle map, with a documented transverse homoclinic intersection.

Exit criteria

Existence, uniqueness, and linear classification
You can check the existence–uniqueness theorem's hypotheses for a given vector field, exhibit an IVP where uniqueness fails, and classify a two-dimensional linear flow — including defective and complex-spectrum cases — directly from its eigendata.
Nonlinear qualitative theory and Lyapunov stability
You can build a defensible phase portrait from nullclines, linearization, and index theory, then certify stability with a constructed Lyapunov function or extend it with LaSalle's invariance principle when decrease is only semidefinite.
Bifurcation and center-manifold reduction
You can continue equilibria through a parameter, locate a saddle-node or Hopf point from an eigenvalue crossing, and reduce a nonhyperbolic system to its center manifold to derive the governing normal form.
Hyperbolicity and ergodic averaging
You can classify a fixed point's stability from spectral separation, approximate the stable and unstable manifolds of a saddle map, and explain when a time average converges to a space average under an invariant measure.
PDE and delay: infinite-dimensional state spaces
You can simulate a reaction–diffusion instability or derive the characteristic equation of a scalar delay model, and pair the numerical result with its analytic stability criterion.
Place in the sequence
This spine builds the flow, linearization, Lyapunov, bifurcation, hyperbolicity, and ergodic machinery; it does not catalog the named systems that instantiate it — the logistic map, the Hénon map, and Smale's horseshoe live in the DSZ guide.

Curation policy

Scope: deterministic finite-dimensional dynamics — existence and uniqueness, linear and nonlinear classification, Lyapunov stability, bifurcation, hyperbolicity, and ergodic averaging, with an entry point into PDE and delay state spaces. Named example systems such as the logistic map, the Hénon map, and Smale's horseshoe are the DSZ guide; randomness and stochastic processes are the STO and SPZ guides; simulation methods in their own right are the SIM guide.
