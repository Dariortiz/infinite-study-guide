Lattices as Mathematics · infinite study guide

Darío's LAT MOOC

From Minkowski bodies to homogeneous spaces.

A video-first spine built from Daniele Micciancio's Simons Institute lattice boot camp, Maryna Viazovska's sphere-packing lecture, Anish Ghosh's homogeneous-dynamics lecture, and Oded Regev's and Vinod Vaikuntanathan's lattice-cryptography surveys, backed by Cassels, Gruber–Lekkerkerker, Stein–Shakarchi, Nguyen–Vallée, Conway–Sloane, and Einsiedler–Ward. The order moves from convex-body geometry through successive minima and duality, Fourier/Gaussian analysis, reduction algorithms, packing and root lattices, and finally homogeneous dynamics. NUM embeds a number field's ring of integers as a lattice to bound its class group; this guide picks up there and treats the lattice as the object of study, then hands SIS/LWE constructions on to PQC and FHE.

vol(K) > 2ⁿdet(Λ) ⇒ K∩Λ≠0
Λ* = {y : ⟨x,y⟩∈ℤ ∀x∈Λ}
Σ_Λ f = 1/det(Λ)·Σ_Λ* f̂
‖b*ᵢ‖² ≤ 2^(i-j)‖b*ⱼ‖²

Minkowski's convex-body theorem forces a short vector to exist from volume alone; Poisson summation exchanges a sum over a lattice for a sum over its dual; LLL turns that existence statement into an algorithm that actually finds a short, size-reduced basis.

Choose your route

There's no single course that carries convex-body existence proofs through duality, Poisson summation, LLL, and homogeneous dynamics without switching fields. Micciancio's Simons Institute boot camp supplies the geometric and algorithmic spine; Viazovska's and Ghosh's invited lectures extend it into packing and dynamics; Regev and Vaikuntanathan mark exactly where the mathematics hands off to PQC and FHE.

EXP

Geometry explorer

Follow Micciancio's boot camp through geometry, minima, Poisson summation, and LLL, add Ghosh's homogeneous-dynamics lecture, then take both the PQC and FHE crypto pointers without their exercises.

9–12 h video + reading
BLD

Balanced builder

Complete every core artifact: convex-body visualizations, dual-lattice calculations, Poisson/theta checks, exact-arithmetic LLL, and a modular-surface diagonal-flow orbit study.

20–30 h + reading
DIP

Deep dive

Add Viazovska's sphere-packing lecture and Conway–Sloane's atlas for E8 and Leech, work through Marklof–Strömbergsson's ICTP research-school lecture on homogeneous dynamics, and keep cryptosystem study in PQC/FHE.

40–60 h + reading

MODULE 00

Geometry of Numbers

Turn volume into arithmetic existence through Blichfeldt, Minkowski's convex-body theorem, lattice determinants, and number-field embeddings.

Mathematics of Lattices
Daniele Micciancio · Simons Institute lattice boot camp · 102 min

A substantial boot-camp lecture develops lattice geometry and its central quantitative parameters from first principles.

After watching: For three planar bases, compute determinant and shortest vectors, then compare the algebraic data with a plot.

Minkowski's Geometry of Numbers
Billy Woods · Algebraic Number Theory lecture · 16 min

A focused proof-level bridge from convex bodies and covolume to arithmetic existence statements.

After watching: Embed a quadratic field as a lattice and derive the numerical Minkowski bound for its ideal classes.
▶ Watch
Cassels, An Introduction to the Geometry of Numbers — Chapters I–III
J. W. S. Cassels · Springer Classics in Mathematics · read

The classical proof spine for fundamental regions, Blichfeldt's lemma, convex bodies, and Minkowski's theorem.

After reading: Prove Blichfeldt's lemma and Minkowski's first theorem, then identify exactly where symmetry and convexity enter.
Read
Exit artifact: Prove Minkowski's first theorem, visualize sharp and non-sharp bodies in dimensions two and three, and apply it to one quadratic field.
MODULE 01

The Mathematics of Lattices I
Vinod Vaikuntanathan · Simons Institute Cryptography Boot Camp · 68 min

The first focused boot-camp part develops bases, minima, dual lattices, and the geometric quantities used by transference bounds.

After watching: Compute successive minima and dual bases for three planar lattices, recording the norm used in every claim.
▶ Watch
Duality in Lattice-Based Cryptography
Léo Ducas · IACR · 63 min

Although motivated by cryptography, the lecture's dual-lattice viewpoint makes primal/dual geometry and transference operational.

After watching: Derive B*=(B^-1)^T, verify Λ**=Λ, and formulate one primal/dual shortest-vector relation.
▶ Watch
Successive Minima-Type Inequalities
Simons Institute for the Theory of Computing · 33 min

A theorem-level lecture directly accompanies the reading's quantitative treatment of successive minima and their inequalities.

After watching: State the bodies and lattice parameters in one successive-minima inequality, then test its scale on three planar examples.
▶ Watch

Peter Gruber & C. G. Lekkerkerker · North-Holland Mathematical Library 37 · read

Develops successive minima, polar bodies, and the quantitative refinements absent from a quick convex-body introduction.

Read
Exit artifact: Compute successive minima and dual bases for planar examples, then prove one transference inequality with all norm choices stated.

▶ Watch

Vinod Vaikuntanathan · Simons Institute Cryptography Boot Camp · 64 min

The second boot-camp part supplies the Fourier and Gaussian machinery connecting a lattice to its dual.

MODULE 03

The LLL Algorithm
Christof Paar · Cryptography 101 · 30 min

A complete algorithmic walkthrough makes size reduction, Lovász swaps, and the reduced-basis output concrete.

After watching: Implement exact-arithmetic LLL and log every coefficient reduction, swap, and potential change.
▶ Watch
Algorithms for Lattice Problems
Daniele Micciancio · Simons Institute · 73 min

Places basis reduction among the central computational lattice problems and clarifies approximation guarantees.

After watching: Compare Gauss reduction and LLL on generated bases, measuring orthogonality defect and shortest-vector quality.
▶ Watch
Nguyen–Vallée, The LLL Algorithm — Chapters 1–2
Phong Q. Nguyen & Brigitte Vallée, eds. · Springer Information Security and Cryptography · read

Treats LLL as a mathematical algorithm with invariants, complexity, and approximation guarantees rather than as an attack button.

A compact mathematical account reinforces why E8's symmetry and modular-form structure certify optimality.

Capstones & exit criteria

A finished route ends in an artifact you can run and a statement you can defend.

Geometry-to-dynamics lattice atlas

  1. Construct and visualize a family of determinant-one planar lattices.
  2. Track minima, duals, theta coefficients, and LLL-reduced bases along a diagonal orbit.
  3. Explain cusp excursions through a Diophantine approximation statement.
  4. Deliver proofs, exact arithmetic, and reproducible figures in one notebook dossier.

Mathematics-to-crypto dependency map

  1. Choose one SIS/LWE or FHE module from the existing guides.
  2. Identify every imported lattice theorem and restate it independently of the cryptosystem.
  3. Demonstrate one theorem computationally on toy lattices without implementing a scheme.
  4. Document the precise point where LAT ends and PQC/FHE begins.

Weekly loop

  • Watch: separate the geometric existence statement from the algorithm that finds it.
  • Derive: reprove one minima, duality, or Poisson-summation identity by hand.
  • Build: implement one lattice computation — determinant, dual basis, LLL, or a root-lattice Gram matrix.
  • Explain: state exactly which prerequisite theorem a PQC or FHE construction is importing.

Minimum portfolio

  • Determinant and successive-minima calculator with a plotted planar-lattice family.
  • Dual-basis and transference-inequality checker.
  • Exact-arithmetic LLL implementation with a logged size-reduction and swap trace.
  • Root-lattice generator (A2, D4, E8) reporting Gram matrix and packing density.
  • Modular-surface diagonal-flow or geodesic-flow simulation.

Exit criteria

Geometry of numbers and successive minima
You can prove Minkowski's convex-body theorem, compute successive minima and dual bases for a planar lattice, and derive one transference inequality with every norm stated.
Gaussians, Poisson summation, and reduction
You can verify Poisson summation numerically, derive the Gaussian theta transformation, and implement exact-arithmetic LLL with its size-reduction and swap steps logged.
Packing and root lattices
You can construct A2, D4, and E8 from generators, compute their Gram matrices and minimum norms, and relate those to packing density and kissing number.
Homogeneous dynamics
You can identify SL(2,R)/SL(2,Z) with unimodular planar lattices, describe the diagonal-flow action, and connect cusp excursions to Diophantine approximation through the Dani correspondence.
Place in the sequence
You can point to where NUM's ideal-class-group computation feeds this guide through Minkowski's bound, and where this guide's geometry and Gaussian machinery hands off to SIS/LWE constructions in PQC and to noise and ring structure in FHE — without re-deriving either scheme here.
